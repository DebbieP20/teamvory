The problem

From text messages and Excel to one place

Today
  1. Employees text their availability.
  2. The owner types it into Excel.
  3. He compares hours to keep things fair.
  4. He builds the schedule cell by cell.
With TeamVory
  1. Employees set a default availability once and edit only the weeks that change.
  2. The owner picks this month's shift pattern.
  3. He clicks Generate, or assigns shifts himself.
  4. He publishes, and everyone gets an email.

Shift configuration

Shift patterns that change with the season

Shift times follow sales, so the owner saves named patterns and applies one to a week. Copying last week is a single click, and editing one day never changes the other days or the saved pattern.

How “fair” is calculated

Each person gets a target share of the week's hours based on how many of this week's shift hours they can work. Someone available twice as much gets about twice the hours.

eligibleHours(person) = shift hours this week the person can fully cover target(person) = hoursNeeded × eligibleHours(person) ÷ Σ eligibleHours(everyone)

Only employees share hours this way. The owner is a backup: he puts himself on the shifts he wants, and covers any shift nobody else can. His default is available every shift, so he only marks his days off.

Steps

  1. Keep the shifts the owner pinned for himself. Generate and Regenerate never move them.
  2. Work out each employee's target from the hours still needed.
  3. Fill the hardest shifts first, meaning the ones with the fewest people available.
  4. Give each slot to the available person furthest below their target. Ties are broken at random, which is how Regenerate gives a different but equally fair schedule.
  5. Swap pairs of assignments when it brings people closer to their targets.
  6. Give any shift still empty to the owner, marked “Owner covering”, if he's free and not already working then.
  7. Report anything still unfilled, for example “Tue 5–9 PM needs 1 more, nobody available.”

Generate never breaks these: only people available for the whole shift, no overlapping shifts, no inactive employees, no one on time off. When assigning by hand, the manager can add someone marked not available after agreeing with them; the app asks first and tags the shift “agreed”. Overlaps and inactive employees are always blocked.

Shift changes

Swaps and open shifts

Swap with a coworker

  1. An employee picks their shift and a coworker's shift.
  2. The system checks that both people are available and have no overlap.
  3. The coworker accepts, because their own shift changes too.
  4. Depending on the setting, the swap completes or waits for manager approval.

Post an open shift

  1. An employee posts a shift they can't work.
  2. Every eligible coworker sees it under Open shifts.
  3. The first person to pick it up gets it, with no acceptance step.
  4. Until someone picks it up, the original employee is still on the schedule.

If an employee changes availability after a schedule is published, the change is saved and the manager sees a conflict on the dashboard. The published schedule never changes by itself.

Technical design

Architecture

One Next.js app on Vercel serves the public site, the manager area and the employee area. Every change goes through a server action, then a service with the business rules, then a database query that is always limited to one business.

Data model

Database (ERD)

Every record belongs to a business, and every query filters by it. That keeps each business's data separate and leaves room for more businesses later without a redesign. Shift setup (what's needed) is kept apart from schedules (who works), so the manager can regenerate as often as they like without touching the shifts.

Employees are never deleted, only set to inactive, so past schedules and hour reports stay correct. Published schedules are never deleted either; a new version replaces them and the old one stays in history.
